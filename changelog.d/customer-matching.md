- Clienteling (`@karatos/retail`) and connectors (`@karatos/connectors`): recognizes the same customer across channels by their keyed references alone, never a name, email address or phone number. Each message and call is kept as a `customer.touch` signal and each record about one person (profiles, form submissions, loyalty members, reviews, payments, bookings) as a `customer.identity` signal, and references one record names together are linked, so a customer's Shopify orders, Klaviyo profile, Quo and Twilio texts, WhatsApp messages and email become one customer. A new rule, `clienteling.awaiting-reply`, raises a customer whose message has had no answer on any channel for 4 of the store's open hours (Monday to Saturday, 10 to 6, New York time, by default) in the last 14 days: a past buyer on their own, with what they have spent and "high-value" from $5,000, and everyone else together, each citing the messages and orders behind it. Twilio adds `customerRefs` and Gmail adds `customerRefs` for the other side of each message when the instance has a `CUSTOMER_REF_KEY`; Shopify hashes each customer's email address and phone number into `contactRefs` with the new `SHOPIFY_LINK_CUSTOMERS=on`, and reads every customer again once when it is turned on. A phone number written without its country code, such as `(212) 555-0123`, now also gets the reference of its E.164 form, so forms, Zendesk, Gorgias and the booking tools line up with Quo, Twilio, WhatsApp and Klaviyo; the digits-only reference stays first and unchanged, so existing links hold. See [customers waiting on a reply](docs/departments/README.md#customers-waiting-on-a-reply).
