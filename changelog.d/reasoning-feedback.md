- The feedback loop: what the owner does with recommendations now changes what the next reasoning run recommends. A recommendation dismissed in the last `FEEDBACK_COOLDOWN_DAYS` (default 14, 0 to 90; 0 turns it off) isn't raised again for the same rule and subject unless its dollar estimate grew by a quarter or more, it gained an estimate, or its confidence rose by 0.1; `POST /v1/reason` lists what it held back under `heldBack`. Rules ranked over the last 180 days: one with more than half of at least 3 decisions dismissed has its confidence lowered, to 0.6 times when every one was dismissed, and one whose accepted recommendations brought money back in the value ledger is raised 1.1 times, or 1.2 times when they met their estimates. A run's recommendations are ordered by that factor, ties keeping the rules' order. Every recommendation now records its `rule` and a `ranking` of `{ baseConfidence, factor, note }` saying why it ranks as it does. `GET /v1/feedback` shows each rule's accepts, dismissals, overrides, returned cents, subjects cooling down, factor and note. The history is read from memory on every run, so it carries over a restart; Postgres stores `rule` and `ranking` on `recommendations` (migration `20261126000001_recommendation_feedback.sql`). `BrainPipeline`'s `feedback` option takes a partial policy or `false`. See docs/feedback-loop.md.
